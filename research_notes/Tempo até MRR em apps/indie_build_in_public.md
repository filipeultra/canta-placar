# Indie / build-in-public founders and small apps: time to MRR milestones

Scope note: 23 search calls; several primary sites (indiehackers.com, substack, techcrunch.com) did not resolve from this environment, so many figures come from search-result extracts of those pages and from secondary aggregators. Each row is labelled **SR** (self-reported by founder), **SR-verified** (founder-reported with Stripe/RevenueCat screenshot or TrustMRR verification), **2nd** (secondary write-up, estimate or aggregator), or **Est.** (Latka/Starter Story model estimates). Nothing here is audited. "One-time" = the product sells mostly lifetime licences or one-off packages, so "MRR" really means monthly revenue.

## Q1. Case table: 15-25 real cases with dates to first revenue / $1k / $10k / $100k MRR

### Takeaway
Hits with a pre-existing audience or paid distribution (Photo AI, Cal AI, ShipFast, TypingMind) reached $10k/month in weeks to a few months. Maker B2B SaaS without paid ads (Plausible, Bannerbear) took about 1 to 2 years to reach $10k MRR. Indie consumer mobile apps without an audience usually sit between $20 and $1k MRR for many months (Habit Pixel: 8 months to $1k; Just Log and Fitloxx under $300). Most founders behind the hits had a long string of failed products first.

### Inferences
- Months from launch to $10k/month split into three groups: (a) **under 3 months**: an audience or paid acquisition plus an AI wave (Photo AI ~0.75 mo, Cal AI ~1 mo, ShipFast ~1 mo, TypingMind "a few months"); (b) **9 to 24 months**: B2B maker SaaS (FeedbackPanda ≤9 mo to $20k, Plausible ~21 mo from first payment, Bannerbear ~24 mo); (c) **not reached, or years**: indie mobile and slow cases (HabitKit ~2-3 yrs to reach $10k+, Personal Best never in ~5 yrs, Just Log, Fitloxx, the padel coach app).
- $1k MRR: B2B tools with a clear pain point got there in about 1 month (Baremetrics, Buffer's first customers in days) to about 12 months (Plausible). Consumer mobile apps in the sample took about 8 months (Habit Pixel), or got an early spike and then stalled (HabitKit: $400 by month 2, then founder back to his job).
- Every "fast" hit came after years of slow or failed work (Levels: ~4 of 70+; Marc Lou: 17-30 failures; Tony Dinh: ~$600 MRR after ~1-2 years; Danny Postma: Headlime before HeadshotPro). Measured from the founder's first product, time to $10k is usually 2 to 5 years, even for the famous cases.
- Many famous AI "MRR" numbers are actually one-time revenue (ShipFast, HeadshotPro packages, TypingMind licences), so they overstate recurring revenue compared with a subscription app.

### Gaps

- No indie sports-stat or scorekeeping app with published, meaningful MRR was found. Absence of evidence may itself be a signal for the category.
- RevenueCat Sub Club episode transcripts could not be retrieved, so no Sub Club-specific case numbers are included.

## Q2. What is typical: months to $1k and $10k MRR, and what share stalled

### Takeaway
Across all subscription apps, most never reach $1k/month. Only about 17-25% get there within 1-2 years, and only ~3.5% reach $10k. The apps that do reach $1k typically do it fast (median ~60 days). In the case sample, founders with an existing audience reached $10k in under 3 months, B2B maker SaaS took ~9-24 months, and indie mobile consumer apps without a distribution edge mostly stalled under $1k-5k.

### Cited Findings
- RevenueCat 2025 report: ~20-25% of apps reach $1,000 monthly revenue within two years of release; education apps take 79 days on average and shopping 90 — [Gigazine summary](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20250318-mobile-apps-subscriptions-revenue); more than 80% never reach $1,000/month in the first two years — [Sherwood News](https://sherwood.news/tech/data-shows-most-new-apps-dont-just-fail-they-completely-bomb)
- For apps that do reach $1k/month, the median time is 60 days — [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-apps-revenue-developers/)
- Top 5% of new apps make $8,880 after 2 years, vs ≤$19 for the bottom 25% (a >400x gap); photo/video and gaming apps are the most likely to reach $1k — [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-apps-revenue-developers/); [mjtsai](https://mjtsai.com/blog/2025/03/24/mobile-app-revenue-in-2025)
- 2026 report (secondary citation): median monthly revenue after 12 months <$50; 17.2% reach $1k/month; 3.5% reach $10k — [The Swift Kit blog (vendor, secondary)](https://theswiftk.it.com/blog/how-much-do-indie-ios-developers-make)
- Vendor claim: successful indie fitness apps reach $3k-10k MRR in year one at $9.99/mo or $59.99/yr; top performers took 3-5 years to reach $100k+ MRR (no data cited) — [The Swift Kit](https://theswiftk.it.com/ios-app-ideas/fitness)
- Stall examples: Simple Analytics flat after 2022 without marketing — [IH](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/joining-a-technical-co-founder-and-growing-the-product-to-45k-mrr-119Zbnde7pTET0QOiZLH); HabitKit's founder returned to employment 2 months after launch — [brunch.co.kr](https://brunch.co.kr/@ddalkakdiary/50); Levels ~95% failure rate — [DEV](https://dev.to/idonthaveapen/failures-behind-a-420kmonth-solo-founder-i-read-all-751-of-pieter-levels-blog-posts-1h3b)

### Inferences
- A sensible base rate for an indie consumer subscription app: about a 1 in 5 chance of ever reaching $1k/month within 2 years, and about 1 in 30 of reaching $10k. Because the median time to $1k among apps that get there is ~60 days, an app still far from $1k after 6-9 months is statistically in the stalled majority unless something changes (channel, price, positioning).
- In the 24-case sample, about 9 cases (Fitloxx, Just Log, Personal Best, the padel coach app, the padel scoring tools, early HabitKit, early DevUtils/Black Magic, Simple Analytics 2022, Buffer month 1) are slow or stalled phases. The sample is still heavily biased toward hits because public case studies are mostly about winners.
- Health & fitness and habit trackers can work (HabitKit $28k MRR, 1 Better $92k/30d, Gentler Streak), but these took ~2-3 years, Apple featuring, or a lifetime/low-annual pricing hook.

### Gaps
- No primary RevenueCat PDF was retrieved; the 2026 figures (17.2% / 3.5%) come only from a vendor blog.
- No category-specific (sports) time-to-$1k benchmark was found.

## Q3. Lessons: does build in public work as an acquisition channel for B2C consumer apps vs B2B maker tools?

### Takeaway
Build in public works best when the buyers are other makers or tech people who follow founders on X/Indie Hackers (ShipFast, Bannerbear, TypingMind, Baremetrics). It works for B2C mainly as a multiplier of an audience the founder already has (Levels). For consumer mobile apps, the winners in the sample grew through paid or creator social (Cal AI), Apple featuring (Gentler Streak), Product Hunt/ASO and pricing (HabitKit), not through founder build-in-public posts.

### Cited Findings
- Bannerbear's early community from "transparent building" on Twitter/IH helped carry it to $10k MRR — [Gildre](https://gildre-for-founders.beehiiv.com/p/the-gildre-april-founder-newsletter-how-founders-get-their-first-10k-mrr)
- Marc Lou "shipped it live, shared his revenue, took feedback in public" and built an audience across 10-15 small launches before ShipFast — [Peerlist](https://peerlist.io/vi_c0de/articles/make-fast-ship-fast-the-relentless-engine-behind-marc-lou); [Tiny Empires](https://tinyempires.substack.com/p/case-study-the-journey-of-marc-lou)
- Baremetrics built in public (open revenue dashboard), reached $100k MRR and burned out anyway — [We Are Founders](https://www.wearefounders.uk/how-baremetrics-built-in-public-hit-100k-mrr-and-burned-out-anyway/)
- Tony Dinh had ~8,000 X followers and ~$600 MRR in Aug 2021. The audience compounded when TypingMind arrived in 2023 — [IH](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/my-solopreneur-story-zero-to-45k-mo-in-2-years-1b858f47af)
- Cal AI grew through social media marketing tests (starting at $2k), not founder build-in-public — [unwire.hk](https://unwire.hk/2025/09/15/teen-ceo-ai-calorie-app-success/software/)
- Gentler Streak: Apple editorial (Heart Month 2022, Watch App of the Year 2022, ADA finalist 2023) — [Leisure Opportunities](https://www.leisureopportunities.co.uk/news/Kind-activity-tracker-Gentler-Streak-wins-Apple-Watch-App-of-the-Year/350559)
- Plausible grew without paid ads, through content and positioning (a privacy angle against Google Analytics) — [Plausible blog](https://ingest.plausible.io/blog/bootstrapping-saas)
- Simple Analytics: a viral HN launch gave first customers, but growth stalled once nobody did marketing — [IH](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/joining-a-technical-co-founder-and-growing-the-product-to-45k-mrr-119Zbnde7pTET0QOiZLH)

### Inferences
- Build-in-public followers on X are mostly makers. That matches the buyers of dev/maker tools, but not amateur athletes or other mainstream consumers. For a B2C sports app, build in public is better used for credibility, feedback, a press/launch spike (Product Hunt, IH) and recruiting early fans than as the main growth engine.
- Consumer mobile winners combine (1) a short-form video / creator loop (Cal AI), (2) ASO plus Apple featuring (Gentler Streak, HabitKit), and (3) pricing hooks such as lifetime or cheap annual plans (HabitKit, 1 Better). Product-native sharing (shareable scorecards and highlight clips) is the B2C version of "building in public": the users do the posting, not the founder.
- Launch spikes decay (HabitKit's Product Hunt launch, Simple Analytics' HN launch, Buffer's HN launch). Every case that kept growing added a repeatable channel within months: content/SEO, ASO, paid social or integrations.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study was found comparing build in public's conversion for B2C vs B2B. The conclusions above come from the case pattern, not measured attribution.
- No Sub Club / RevenueCat interview transcripts on build in public for consumer apps were retrieved.
